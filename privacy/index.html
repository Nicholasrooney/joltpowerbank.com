This notice explains how Jolt Ireland Technology Ltd ("Jolt", "we") uses information collected through this website. Last updated: November 2026.

Who we are

Jolt Ireland Technology Ltd, [company number], [registered office address]. Contact: [privacy contact email] or WhatsApp +353 87 036 5888.

Information you give us

When you ask for a station or join the waitlist, we collect your business name, your name, email, phone number and, for EU or international requests, your country, together with the answers you choose in the form (number of venues, weekly customers, recommended station).

We use this only to contact you about a Jolt station or about expanding to your area. Our legal basis is taking steps at your request before entering into a contract, and our legitimate interest in responding to business enquiries. We keep enquiries for up to 24 months unless you become a partner venue, then for as long as the partnership lasts and as required by law.

Website statistics without cookies

Our own server counts page views and actions on the site (for example which buttons are tapped and which step of the form is reached). It sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device. To count daily visitors, it combines your IP address and browser type with a random value that is replaced every day, and stores only the result. That result cannot identify you and cannot be linked across days. Your IP address itself is not stored. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in understanding and improving the website.

Who we share information with

  • Our website host (Hostinger), which stores the site and its statistics database.
  • WhatsApp (Meta), if you send us a message or your enquiry is passed to us by WhatsApp.
  • Google, for analytics, only if you accept cookies. Google may process data outside the EEA under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

We do not sell your information.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete your information, object to how we use it, or ask us to stop contacting you, by contacting us as above. You can also complain to the Data Protection Commission (dataprotection.ie) or, in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).
